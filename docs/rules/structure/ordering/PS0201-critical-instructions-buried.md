# PS0201 — Critical instructions buried

## Summary

Important instructions—such as safety constraints, output contracts, or high-priority goals—are buried in the middle of a long prompt instead of being made prominent (for example, near the end or in a clearly marked section).  
This increases the risk that the model will implicitly down-weight them, overlook them, or conflict with later, less important instructions.

## Rationale

Empirical work on long-context behavior suggests that models may underweight or forget content that appears in the middle of long contexts, and developer experience reflects similar behavior.  
When critical instructions are embedded in dense prose or mixed with low-importance details, the model is more likely to miss them, and humans reviewing the prompt are more likely to miss them as well.  
Bringing these instructions to the top or bottom, or into clearly labeled sections, makes prompts more robust and easier to maintain.

## Detection guidance

Detectors SHOULD consider flagging PS010 when:

- The prompt is relatively long (for example, multiple paragraphs or sections).
- One or more sentences include strong modal phrases or critical constraints, such as:
  - “Never…”, “Always…”, “Do not…”, “You must…”, “Under no circumstances…”.
  - Definitions of the output contract (“Return JSON only…”, “Respond in English only…”).
- Those sentences appear:
  - In the middle third of the prompt, not near the beginning or end.
  - Inside dense paragraphs that are otherwise descriptive or low priority (background, anecdotes, etc.).

Heuristics:

- Segment the prompt into logical blocks (headings, paragraphs, bullet lists).
- Identify “critical” sentences using keyword patterns or model-based classification.
- If a critical sentence is surrounded by non-instructional text and there is no summarizing “Rules” or “Instructions” section at the end, the rule likely applies.

This rule is more subjective than PS001–PS003, so detectors SHOULD be conservative and MAY require multiple signals before emitting a violation.

## Repair guidance

Because the relative ordering of sections can matter, this rule is best repaired with human-in-the-loop assistance:

- Extract critical instructions into a dedicated “Rules”, “Constraints”, or “Important instructions” section.
- Move that section to a prominent position—either right after the task statement or at the very end of the prompt.
- Optionally, emphasize critical items with bullets or numbering rather than hiding them in paragraphs.

Example repair:

- From:  
  > You are a helpful assistant…  
  > [two long paragraphs of background]  
  > Under no circumstances should you reveal internal API keys or secrets.  
  > [more background and notes]

- To:  
  > You are a helpful assistant…  
  > [background]  
  >
  > **Important instructions:**  
  > 1. Under no circumstances should you reveal internal API keys or secrets.  
  > 2. If a user asks for credentials, explain that you cannot provide them and suggest safer alternatives.  
  >  
  > [remaining background or examples]

Automated tools MAY propose a patch that lifts detected critical sentences into a separate section, but they SHOULD avoid changing wording without human approval.

## Limitations

- Not all “critical” instructions are equally important; static analysis cannot always distinguish between must-follow rules and minor preferences.
- Some prompts intentionally interleave instructions with examples; naive extraction can disrupt those examples and degrade performance.
- In short prompts, “middle” versus “end” distinctions are not meaningful; PS010 SHOULD be disabled below a length threshold.

Implementations SHOULD allow configuration of:

- What qualifies as “critical” (keyword lists, severity levels).
- Minimum prompt length for PS010 to apply.
- Whether to treat headings like “Rules” or “Safety” as sufficient even if they are not at the very end.

## Examples

### Non-compliant

> You are a research assistant helping summarize internal security documents.  
>
> Our company has a long history of security best practices… [long background]  
> Under no circumstances should you mention that this summary was generated by an AI system.  
> [more background and context]

The critical constraint is buried in the middle of descriptive text.

### Compliant

> You are a research assistant helping summarize internal security documents.  
>
> **Important instructions:**  
>
> - Under no circumstances should you mention that this summary was generated by an AI system.  
> - If you are unsure, prefer to omit speculative details rather than invent them.  
>  
> [background and context]

## Evidence notes

- Research on long-context models has shown that content near the middle of long inputs can be less reliably used than content near the beginning or end.  
- Practitioner reports and security research on prompt fuzzing also emphasize the importance of making safety constraints prominent rather than burying them.
